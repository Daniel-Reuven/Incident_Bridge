Using Incident Bridge

Incident Bridge tracks your team's IT work in two queues: routine maintenance tasks, done strictly in order, and faults, where the most severe one always comes first. This guide covers everything a user can do, then the extra tools an admin has. Admin-only steps are marked Admin.

Getting started

  • Your account is created for you by an admin — there is no sign-up. Ask your administrator for a username and a first password.
  • At your first login (and after an admin resets your password) you must choose a new password before you can do anything else.
  • Log in on the start page. You stay logged in in that browser for up to 12 hours (your administrator may set another limit), or until you press Log out in the top bar. After 5 wrong passwords in a row the account is locked for 15 minutes.
  • Your username and role (user or admin) are shown in the top bar.

Settings: password and appearance

Press Settings in the top bar.

  • Password — type your current password, the new one, and the new one again. A checklist under the new password shows which rules it already meets; the i button next to it shows the password policy. Changing your password signs you out on every other computer or browser.
  • Interface — the color scheme (Light, Dark, or System, which follows your computer's setting) and the text size (90% to 130%). They apply at once, to every page, and are remembered in this browser.

Password policy

Every password — your own, and one an admin sets for you — must:

  • be 8 to 128 characters long;
  • contain at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter, one digit and one symbol (anything that is not a letter or a digit, for example ! @ # $ % or a space);
  • not contain your username (in any letter case);
  • not be a common password such as P@ssw0rd or Welcome123! — the app keeps a list of them;
  • when you change it, differ from your current password.

A long passphrase is easier to remember and harder to guess than a short jumble, for example Coffee before 9 deploys!. Passwords are stored only as a salted hash, never as text — nobody, not even an admin, can see yours.

The dashboard

After logging in you land on the dashboard. It has four tabs, each with its count: Everything (all of the below), Maintenance (the maintenance queue and the active tasks), Faults (the fault queue and the active faults) and Archive (closed incidents of both kinds, most recently closed first). The Everything tab, from top to bottom:

  • Maintenance queue — first in, first out: tasks are done one at a time, in the order they were reported. #1 in queue is next.
  • Fault queue — priority order: every Critical fault before any Major one, and every Major before any Minor; within a severity, oldest first.
  • All incidents — everything, including closed incidents. The Type, Status and Sort by filters at the top apply to this list.

Type in Filter to show only incidents whose title, description or comments contain the words you type (a row found only through a comment says match in comments); the tab counts then show matching / total.

Click any row to open the incident. The dashboard updates by itself when anyone changes something — a closed incident moves to Archive on its own — there is no need to refresh. Coming back from an incident page reopens the same tab, with the same filter.

Two helpers

  • Check stale incidents highlights in-progress incidents with no update for the time chosen in Stale after (default 4 hours). Not shown on the Archive tab.
  • Pressing maintenance calls (Maintenance tab) lists open maintenance tasks that have waited more than 3 days, oldest first. Show first limits the list.

Reporting an incident

  1. On the dashboard, press New incident.
  2. Choose the Kind: Maintenance task for planned, routine work, or Fault / issue for something that is broken.
  3. Give it a short Title and a Description of what you see.
  4. For a fault, answer the four severity questions — System is unavailable, Security breach, Performance degraded and Affected users (%). The app sets the severity from your answers:
    • unavailable or a security breach → Critical
    • performance degraded, or at least 25% of users affected → Major
    • anything else → Minor
  5. Press Create. A maintenance task joins the back of the maintenance queue; a fault takes its place in the fault queue by severity.

Working maintenance tasks

  • Start next (dashboard) or Start (on the task's page) begins the first task in line. Tasks start strictly in order, and only one can be in progress at a time.
  • When the work is done, open the task and press Complete, with a short closing message.
  • A task that is no longer needed can be closed from any place in the queue with Close task (a reason is required); the tasks behind it move up one place.

Working faults

  • Claim next (dashboard) takes the most severe fault that is waiting and assigns it to you.
  • On a fault's own page, Claim works for any fault in the most severe group that is waiting. If more severe faults are still waiting, the button is disabled and tells you how many.
  • Post what you find as updates in the box under Updates (up to 5,000 characters; the counter shows how many you have used). Order shows the newest or the oldest first — your browser remembers the choice.
  • When it is fixed, press Resolve and write what was done (up to 1,000 characters). A fault can also be resolved while it is still waiting, without claiming it.
Every status change — claimed, closed, reopened — is written into the incident's updates automatically, with who did it and why, so the history is always complete.

AI analysis of a fault

When the server has an AI provider set up, open and in-progress faults show an Analyze using <provider> button. The AI reads the fault and all its updates and posts its view — a summary, the likely cause, evidence, next steps, missing information and a severity check — as a comment by ai-assistant.

  1. Press Analyze using … — a comment records that you asked, and the button shows the request's place: Queued (position 2), then Analyzing…. Analyses run one at a time, so this can take a few minutes.
  2. While it waits, anyone can press Cancel analysis.
  3. The answer appears in the updates. A long answer is split into up to three parts; if the AI could not answer, an error comment says why.
  • A fault can have only one analysis waiting at a time.
  • After a successful analysis, the button is greyed out (No changes since the last analysis) until someone changes the fault — a new update, a status or severity change — because the answer would be the same.
  • Admin Re-analyze runs it again without changes, once a cooldown has passed since the last analysis (5 minutes by default); the button counts down until then.
The fault and its updates are sent to the AI provider (Google, Anthropic or OpenAI, as configured). Do not put passwords, keys or personal data in incidents — see the Terms.

Admin tools Admin

  • Not an incident and By design close an incident with that reason, instead of Resolve. A closing message is still required.
  • Severity — on an in-progress fault, the Severity menu changes it; the fault's place in the queue follows.
  • Reopen… on a closed incident, with a reason. A maintenance task asks where in the queue it goes back; a fault goes back to the queue (Open) or to you (In progress). The earlier resolution stays in the history.
  • Import seed data (dashboard) loads incidents from a .jsonl file; incidents already there are skipped, not duplicated.
  • Re-analyze — see AI analysis.

Users Admin

Users in the top bar lists every account.

  • New user — a username (3–32 letters, digits, ., _ or -), a role and a first password that follows the policy. Give the password to the person; they must change it at their first login.
  • Make admin / Make user changes the role.
  • Disable signs the person out at once and stops them from logging in; Enable lets them back in. Accounts are never deleted, so their incidents and comments keep their author.
  • Reset password sets a new password for someone who forgot theirs; it signs them out everywhere and they must change it at their next login.
  • You cannot change your own role or disable yourself — use Settings for your own password.

The site portal Admin

The Site portal button in the top bar watches the organization's websites and tells the right people when one has a problem.

  • Check all sites, or Check on one site, tests whether each site answers. One failed check marks it Degraded; two in a row, Down — so a single dropped request never raises an outage. Status sets a status by hand, with a reason (Maintenance is only set this way).
  • The summary at the top shows the most urgent site and anything that needs attention: outages no incident mentions, sites that look fine although incidents mention them, sites no mailing list covers, lists with no members.
  • Mailing lists say who is told about which sites.
  • Notifications: each meaningful status change creates a draft to the lists covering that site. Open it with Review, edit the text if needed, then Send to lists or Dismiss.
  • Archive removes a site or list from use; the Archive tab can Restore it.
  • Sent, dismissed and skipped notifications move to the Archive tab after a set time (30 days unless the operator changed it), where they can only be viewed. Notifications waiting for a decision stay in Notifications.

Good to know

  • Live updates: lists update by themselves. If someone changes the incident you are looking at, a prompt offers to refresh it.
  • Server restarts: open pages reconnect on their own within a few seconds, and you stay logged in.
  • Something refused? The message says why — for example, a task that is not first in line cannot be started, and admin-only actions are not offered to users.
